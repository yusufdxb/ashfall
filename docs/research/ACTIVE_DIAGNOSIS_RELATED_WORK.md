## Verdict and consequence (read first)

**Closed-set safe active discrimination is prior art.** Active fault diagnosis with auxiliary
inputs designed to separate candidate models is decades old (Nikoukhah 1998; Campbell and
Nikoukhah; Scott et al. 2014 constrained zonotopic AFD, DOI 10.1016/j.automatica.2014.03.016).
Choosing robot actions that maximize disagreement among candidate models is Bongard, Zykov and
Lipson, Science 2006 (DOI 10.1126/science.1133687). Most important: **Ni, Ornik, Chou and
Coogan, "Safe, Real-Time Active Model Discrimination and Fault Diagnosis for Nonlinear Systems
via Differentiable Reachability", arXiv:2606.19590 (June 2026, preprint)**: "Given a finite set
of candidate models representing nominal and faulty modes, including actuator and sensor faults,
we formulate an output-feedback, time-varying policy optimization problem that (i) robustly
enforces state-input safety constraints over a finite horizon and (ii) drives the system to
produce sampled measurements consistent with at most one model, enabling deterministic
diagnosis." Experiments include hardware and quadrupedal navigation. Its safety machinery
(differentiable reachability, robust guarantees) is far stronger than anything in this toy.

**What that paper and the others do not do**, per the agent's full-text search and my check of the
abstract: return UNKNOWN when no candidate model explains the data. The candidate set is closed;
diagnosis is forced. Also not found: hypotheses that are *simulator-mismatch mechanisms* generated
by a real failure the simulator did not predict (the prior work diagnoses hardware fault modes).

**Adapted question (binding for this study).** Ashfall does not claim safe active discrimination
among known models. It asks only:

> Does safe active probing detect that the mechanism library is inadequate (UNKNOWN) better than
> passive calibrated abstention, an optimized fixed probe and random safe probes, while not
> harming known-mechanism diagnosis?

Known-mechanism accuracy is a non-inferiority requirement, not a claimed contribution. If the
open-set gain is absent, there is nothing left that Ni et al. and the AFD literature do not already
cover, and the study stops.

Caveat: Ni et al. was checked at abstract level by hand and by full-text keyword search by the
agent; the paper must be read in full before any public claim.

### Synthesis

Active fault diagnosis (AFD) with hard safety constraints, model-discrimination-optimal probe design, and even legged-robot hardware validation all exist as separate, well-established lines (classical AFD/auxiliary-signal-design going back to Nikoukhah 1998 and Campbell/Nikoukhah's 2004 book; Bayesian/OED model discrimination from Box and Hill 1967; robot self-modeling from Bongard/Zykov/Lipson 2006; sim-to-real active system ID from ASID 2024 and SPI-Active 2025). The closest single paper is a June 2026 arXiv preprint (Ni, Ornik, Chou, Coogan, "Safe, Real-Time Active Model Discrimination and Fault Diagnosis for Nonlinear Systems via Differentiable Reachability," arXiv:2606.19590) that does safety-constrained active model discrimination on a real Unitree Go2 quadruped. It is close enough that Ashfall's team must read it in full before resuming the project, but it targets discrete hardware/sensor faults from a closed, fixed candidate set with guaranteed (set-based) elimination, not simulator-parameter mismatch mechanisms scored by one-step reset-based residual likelihood, and it has no open-set/UNKNOWN or UNRESOLVED output. No paper found combines all of: (1) trigger from a logged real robot failure, (2) a library of interpretable *simulator* mismatch mechanisms (friction, actuator strength, latency, observation delay) scored by residual likelihood, (3) safety-admissible probe synthesis maximizing hypothesis disagreement under every plausible hypothesis, (4) explicit UNKNOWN (open-set) and UNRESOLVED (no safe separating probe) outputs, run iteratively for up to N probes.

### Close-neighbor notes

**Bongard, Zykov, Lipson (Science 2006)** , read full text (5-page PDF, mirror at meclab.w3.uvm.edu). Confirmed quote: "our approach is based on the concept of multiple competing internal models and generation of actions to maximize disagreement between predictions of these models... searching for the actuation pattern that, when executed on each of the candidate self-models, causes the most disagreement across the predicted sensor signals." This is architecturally the ancestor of step (2)-(3) of Ashfall (disagreement-maximizing probe design over a candidate-model set), explicitly citing query-by-committee (Seung, Opper, Sompolinsky 1992). Differences from Ashfall: (a) candidate models are an open continuous space of robot topologies/limb-length parameters synthesized by stochastic optimization, not a fixed interpretable library of named simulator-mismatch mechanisms (friction, actuator strength, latency, observation delay); (b) no admissibility/hard-safety constraint on the exploratory action , actions are chosen purely to maximize disagreement, with no notion of "safe under every plausible hypothesis"; (c) no UNKNOWN/open-set or UNRESOLVED output , the algorithm always terminates with "the most accurate model," never declares the library inadequate or that no safe separating probe exists; (d) target is the robot's own morphology after physical damage, not disambiguating which of several simulator-vs-reality mismatch mechanisms explains an observed sim-to-real failure trace.

**ASID (arXiv:2404.12308) and SPI-Active (arXiv:2505.14266)** , both read via abstract; SPI-Active abstract explicitly: "we introduce an active exploration strategy that maximizes the Fisher Information of the collected real-world trajectories via optimizing the input commands of an exploration policy," tested on legged locomotion tasks. Both are continuous-parameter system identification (mass, friction-like physical parameters) driven by an initial simulator, executed as a data-collection phase to *refine* the simulator before deployment, not triggered by an observed failure and not choosing among discrete, named, interpretable mismatch-mechanism hypotheses. Neither reports hard safety admissibility constraints on the exploration policy, nor an open-set/UNKNOWN output, nor an UNRESOLVED output when no safe probe exists.

**Ni, Ornik, Chou, Coogan (arXiv:2606.19590, June 2026)** , read full HTML text via arxiv.org/html. This is the closest available paper on safety-constrained active probe synthesis for hypothesis discrimination on a physical legged robot (real Unitree Go2). Quoted from the paper: "a carefully chosen turning maneuver may generate sensor measurements that disambiguate the failure mode. This motivates active fault diagnosis (AFD): rather than passively monitoring outputs, we design control inputs or policies that deliberately excite the system to make the true model identifiable," and it "robustly enforces state-input safety constraints over a finite horizon" while driving the system to "produce sampled measurements consistent with at most one model, enabling deterministic diagnosis." It reports a hardware quadruped experiment distinguishing "actuator fault" vs "sensor fault" (degraded leg vs biased IMU) using a computed separating controller, diagnosing in 2 seconds. Differences from Ashfall, verified by full-text search (no hits for "unknown," "abstain," or "open-set" anywhere in the paper text): (a) the candidate set is a small, closed, enumerated list of hardware fault modes (sensor/actuator faults, up to 11), not a library of simulator-model-mismatch mechanisms (friction/sliding-friction/braking-friction/actuator-strength/latency/observation-delay) meant to explain a *sim-to-real* discrepancy; (b) diagnosis is framed as continuous/online monitoring plus a single separating-controller synthesis step, not a pipeline explicitly triggered by a specific logged failure trace scored first by one-step reset-based residual likelihood; (c) it uses deterministic/guaranteed set-based separation (reachability), not a Bayesian divergence measure (Bhattacharyya/JS/expected information gain) with iterative weight updates across up to N probes; (d) there is no UNKNOWN output for "no library model predicts the observations" and no UNRESOLVED output for "no safe separating probe exists" , the framework assumes the true mode is one of the enumerated candidates and always attempts a separating controller. Because this is a preprint (posted 2026-06-17, not yet confirmed peer-reviewed/published), its formal reachability-safety machinery is the most directly relevant prior art for Ashfall's step-2/3 "safe probe under every plausible hypothesis" requirement and should be treated as a required citation and point of technical comparison, not a collision, given the closed-set/no-abstention framing confirmed above.

### Verdict (conservative)

**Established, not novel to Ashfall:** (a) active/optimal-input fault diagnosis with information- or separation-maximizing probes is a mature control-theory field (Nikoukhah 1998 onward, DOI 10.1515/9781400880041, 108+ cites; Scott et al. 2014, DOI 10.1016/j.automatica.2014.03.016, 183 cites); (b) choosing actions to maximize disagreement among a candidate model set is established for robot self-modeling since Bongard/Zykov/Lipson, *Science* 2006 (525 cites), quote above; (c) hard, formally-safe active model discrimination has now been demonstrated on a real quadruped by Ni/Ornik/Chou/Coogan (arXiv:2606.19590, June 2026).

**No direct duplicate found.** No paper in the searched literature (i) triggers from a specific logged robot failure trace, (ii) uses a passive one-step reset-based residual-likelihood scoring stage over an explicit library of interpretable *simulator*-mismatch mechanisms (friction, sliding friction, braking friction, actuator strength, latency, observation delay) as step 1, (iii) synthesizes a hard-safety-admissible probe that maximizes an information-theoretic divergence (Bhattacharyya/JS/EIG) between remaining hypotheses specifically to disambiguate *which physics parameter the simulator got wrong* (as opposed to which of a fixed hardware fault-mode list is present), (iv) runs this iteratively up to N probes with weight updates, and (v) exposes explicit KNOWN / UNKNOWN (open-set / library-inadequate) / UNRESOLVED (no safe separating probe) / ABORT outputs.

**Narrow gap that survives:** the combination of (a) a simulator-mismatch-mechanism library (rather than a hardware-fault-mode library) as the hypothesis space, (b) a residual-likelihood passive pre-screen before deciding whether to probe at all, and (c) explicit UNKNOWN/UNRESOLVED open-set abstention alongside hard safety-admissibility under every hypothesis. Ni et al. 2026 is the closest technical precedent for the safety-constrained active-discrimination machinery on a real legged robot and must be read in full and cited/differentiated against before Ashfall resumes (project is currently DORMANT per prior notes); Bongard 2006 is the closest precedent for "action maximizes disagreement across a candidate model set" as an idea, applied to a different problem (robot self-morphology, not simulator mismatch) with no safety constraint and no abstention.

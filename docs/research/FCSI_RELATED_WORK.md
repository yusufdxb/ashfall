## Gate 1 verdict: PASS, conditional and narrow

**No direct duplicate was found.** Every ingredient of FCSI exists separately:

| ingredient | prior art |
|---|---|
| infer a posterior over dynamics parameters from **one failure** plus nominal data | **CalNF** (Dawson, Tran, Li, Fan 2025, arXiv:2502.21110, preprint): "a much smaller set of target observations ... Nt << N0"; UAV case uses "1 failure trajectory"; learns "the posterior distribution ... from sparse failure data" |
| search simulator parameters for failures, then repair | RADIUM (Dawson, Parashar, Fan, IEEE T-RO 2025, DOI 10.1109/tro.2025.3551198) |
| real data, patch the simulator, retrain the policy, on a legged/humanoid robot | **ASAP** (He et al., RSS 2025, arXiv:2502.01143): delta-action residual, fine-tunes policies "with the delta action model integrated into the simulator"; no interpretable mechanisms, not failure-conditioned |
| posterior over simulator parameters from real trajectories | SimOpt, BayesSim, BayRn, NPDR, **DROPO** (one-step, reset-to-real-state likelihood) |
| avoid post-divergence trajectory error | multiple shooting (classical), DROPO's reset-based transitions |
| sparse selection from a named library with a complexity penalty | SINDy; Bayesian model selection; multiple-model fault diagnosis (FDI) |
| which physical mechanism explains a sim-to-real gap | COMPASS (arXiv:2306.15864, preprint, causal graph, manipulation) |

What was **not** found as one piece of work: starting from a real failure the simulator did not
predict; a first-divergence time calibrated against nominal real/sim discrepancy; a sparse
choice among named mechanism families scored on reproducing the failure event (not only
trajectory error); an explicit "unexplained" abstention; then a simulator patch and policy
retraining, on a legged robot.

Consequences, binding on everything after this file:

1. The contribution can only be **the combination plus an empirical finding**, not a new
   primitive. CalNF must be cited as the nearest idea (failure-conditioned posterior), ASAP as the
   nearest loop (patch and retrain on a legged robot), DROPO and multiple shooting as the answer
   to "trajectory MSE is bad after divergence".
2. The empirical claim is only interesting if FCSI beats **strong** baselines, not a strawman:
   whole-trajectory fitting alone is too weak. The toy must include a reset-based multi-step
   prediction-error fit (multiple shooting) and a DROPO-like one-step likelihood fit over the same
   library and the same data. If either matches FCSI on failure reproduction and mechanism
   identification, the central claim fails, whatever FCSI does against whole-trajectory MSE.
3. Verification depth: CalNF and ASAP were checked at abstract/HTML level, RADIUM and COMPASS at
   abstract level. Their appendices were not read line by line. Before any public claim, read them
   in full; a match found there reopens Gate 1.

## Related-work check: FCSI (failure-critical system identification) vs sim-to-real system identification / calibration literature

Scope: system identification and sim-to-real calibration methods; verified via arXiv API, Crossref, and (where OpenAlex/S2 were rate-limited) WebSearch-plus-primary-source WebFetch of the actual paper page. All IDs below were returned by an API call or fetched directly; none were recalled from memory without verification. OpenAlex was rate-limited for the remainder of the day for this network (`"Insufficient budget... resets at midnight UTC"`) after the first few calls, so Crossref + arXiv + direct WebFetch of abstract pages were used as the verification path for several entries; this is noted per-entry.

### Verified papers

1. **Closing the Sim-to-Real Loop: Adapting Simulation Randomization with Real World Experience (SimOpt)** , Chebotar, Handa, Makoviychuk, Macklin, Issac, Ratliff, Fox , **ICRA 2019** , PEER-REVIEWED , `id: arXiv:1810.05687`, `DOI: 10.1109/icra.2019.8793789` (confirmed via Crossref, container-title "2019 International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA)"). Abstract (fetched): uses "a few real world roll-outs interleaved with policy training" to adapt "the simulation parameter distribution" so as to "match the policy behavior in simulation and the real world." Tested on **manipulation** (swing-peg-in-hole, cabinet drawer opening), not legged. No failure-specific term, no divergence-time localization, no mechanism-family selection mentioned in the abstract; objective is trajectory/behavior matching, not event reproduction.

2. **DROPO: Sim-to-Real Transfer with Offline Domain Randomization** , Tiboni, Arndt, Kyrki (co-authors similar) , originally arXiv 2201.08434, journal version **Robotics and Autonomous Systems, Aug 2023** , PEER-REVIEWED , `id: arXiv:2201.08434`, `DOI: 10.1016/j.robot.2023.104432` (confirmed via Crossref). Abstract (fetched): estimates a **distribution over simulator dynamics parameters** from "a limited, precollected offline dataset of trajectories" using a likelihood-based objective; does not require live interaction. Manipulation-focused ("two zero-shot sim-to-real transfer scenarios"); no failure-specific mechanism, no divergence-time localization, no mechanism-family library, no event-reproduction term evident in the abstract.

3. **BayesSim: adaptive domain randomization via probabilistic inference for robotics simulators** , Ramos, Possas, Fox , **RSS 2019** , PEER-REVIEWED , `id: arXiv:1906.01728`. Full likelihood-free (simulation-based) Bayesian inference over simulator parameters from state-action trajectories; posterior used for domain randomization. Classical control / robotics benchmarks, not specifically legged-robot failure cases; whole-trajectory summary statistics, no event-conditioning, no mechanism-family selection or sparsity penalty.

4. **Data-Efficient Domain Randomization with Bayesian Optimization (BayRn)** , Muratore, Eilers, Gienger, Peters , **IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters, April 2021** , PEER-REVIEWED , `id: arXiv:2003.02471`, `DOI: 10.1109/lra.2021.3052391` (confirmed via Crossref). Bayesian-optimization search over a low-dimensional domain-parameter distribution family to maximize real-world policy return; treats the whole rollout as the signal, not a specific failure event; no mechanism-family search or sparsity penalty; not confirmed tested on legged hardware from title/venue alone (not independently fetched full text here).

5. **Neural Posterior Domain Randomization (NPDR)** , Muratore, Gruner, Wiese, Belousov, Gienger, Peters , **CoRL 2021, published PMLR v164, pp. 1532-1542 (2022)** , PEER-REVIEWED , `id: openreview.net/forum?id=59aUaAbVfMA`, proceedings page `proceedings.mlr.press/v164/muratore22a.html` (both located via WebSearch and cross-checked against the ML Anthology CoRL-2021 index; arXiv API search for the exact title returned zero entries, so no arXiv id is claimed , CoRL/PMLR is the peer-reviewed record). Per the paper's own project page description (WebSearch summary, not independently full-text-verified by me): alternates policy learning in a randomized sim with Bayesian posterior updates over simulator parameters from "a small set of real-world observations," using neural likelihood-free (simulation-based) inference; domain parameters need not be independent or Gaussian. No indication of failure-specific conditioning, divergence-time localization, or mechanism-family selection in the description found. NOTE: I did not fetch the PMLR PDF directly, so this entry is WEAKER-VERIFIED than the others (existence and venue are confirmed; the detailed content description is WebSearch-summary-level, not primary-source-quote-level).

6. **ASAP: Aligning Simulation and Real-World Physics for Learning Agile Humanoid Whole-Body Skills** , He et al. , **RSS 2025** , PEER-REVIEWED , `id: arXiv:2502.01143`, `DOI: 10.15607/rss.2025.xxi.066` (confirmed via Crossref). Abstract + fetch: two-stage framework; pretrains motion-tracking policy in sim, deploys on **Unitree G1 humanoid** (legged/whole-body), collects real data, trains a **generic learned delta (residual) action model** that is folded back into the simulator, then **fine-tunes/retrains the policy** with the corrected sim. This is the closest neighbor on the "patch sim + retrain policy" loop and on legged/humanoid hardware. Confirmed NOT doing: failure/divergence-event detection, t_div localization, or selection among a library of interpretable named mechanisms (friction, Kp/Kd, latency, etc.) , it is a black-box residual network learned from tracking error, not a sparse, interpretable, mechanism-selecting posterior. No event-reproduction loss; standard tracking-error reduction is the stated objective.

7. **What Went Wrong? Closing the Sim-to-Real Gap via Differentiable Causal Discovery (COMPASS)** , Huang, Zhang, Cao, Liu, Xu, Ding, Francis, Chen, Zhao , **PREPRINT** (arXiv only; no published-venue DOI found via Crossref bibliographic search) , `id: arXiv:2306.15864`. Learns a differentiable mapping from environment parameters to the sim-real trajectory gap, guided by a learned **causal graph** over parameters , closest thing found in this search to "which mechanism/parameter is causally responsible for the gap." Confirmed (via fetch): applied to **manipulation** tasks, not legged robots; does not explicitly identify failure cases where sim predicted success and reality failed; uses trajectory-alignment style objective, not event-conditioned/failure-conditioned calibration; no t_div localization or abstain option.

8. **Learning agile and dynamic motor skills for legged robots** (actuator network) , Hwangbo, Lee, Dosovitskiy, Bellicoso, Tsounis, Koltun, Hutter , **Science Robotics, 2019** , PEER-REVIEWED , `id: arXiv:1901.08652`, `DOI: 10.1126/scirobotics.aau5872` (confirmed via Crossref/OpenAlex). Learns a neural actuator network as a data-driven replacement for an idealized actuator model in the ANYmal simulator to close the sim-to-real gap for locomotion; this is a single global learned correction (not sparse/interpretable, not failure-conditioned, not selecting among named mechanism families), used once as a standing simulator improvement, not as a per-failure diagnostic loop.

9. **Discovering governing equations from data by sparse identification of nonlinear dynamical systems (SINDy)** , Brunton, Proctor, Kutz , **PNAS, 2016** , PEER-REVIEWED , `DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1517384113` (confirmed via Crossref; 4387 citations at query time). Origin of the "sparse regression over a library of candidate terms, penalized for complexity" idea that FCSI's step 3 (sparsest mechanism family that explains the divergence) draws on structurally. Not robotics-specific, not failure-conditioned, not applied to sim-to-real calibration or legged robots; operates on a library of algebraic/differential terms, not physically-named simulator mechanism families (friction, Kp/Kd, latency, etc.), and has no event-reproduction or abstain concept.

10. **Sparse identification of evolution equations via Bayesian model selection** , arXiv 2025 , checked (arXiv:2501.01476) , PREPRINT , combines thresholded sparse regression with Bayesian-optimization-tuned hyperparameters; applied to fMRI and fluid-flow data, not robotics, not failure-conditioned. Not a close neighbor; included only to confirm no robotics-flavored "sparse Bayesian model selection over mechanism library" preprint already exists under this exact framing.

11. **Parameter Estimation for Chaotic Systems** , **1991 American Control Conference** , PEER-REVIEWED (conference proceedings) , `DOI: 10.23919/acc.1991.4791688` (confirmed via Crossref). Classical justification for multiple-shooting/segmented parameter estimation over single-shooting trajectory MSE in chaotic/divergent systems , the standard citation behind FCSI's implicit claim that full-trajectory MSE is a poor objective post-divergence. Old, general dynamical-systems literature, not robotics- or failure-specific, and does not localize a "first divergence time" against a calibrated nominal-trajectory discrepancy distribution the way FCSI proposes.

### Verdict

No paper found combines all five FCSI elements: (a) failure-triggered replay with a statistically calibrated first-divergence-time detector, (b) search over a small library of interpretable, physically named mechanism families, (c) an explicit sparsity/complexity penalty over that library, (d) an event-reproduction term requiring the failure itself (not just trajectory MSE) to be reproduced, plus an abstain option, and (e) closing the loop by patching sim and retraining policy, on a legged robot.

Individual pieces are each already established, separately:
- Sim-parameter posterior estimation from real data: SimOpt, DROPO, BayesSim, BayRn, NPDR , all peer-reviewed, none failure-conditioned, none mechanism-family-selecting, none localizing t_div.
- Closing the loop (learn correction, patch sim, retrain policy) on a legged/humanoid robot: **ASAP (RSS 2025)** does exactly this loop end-to-end on hardware, but with a generic black-box residual/delta-action model, not a small interpretable mechanism library, no divergence-time localization, no failure-specific/event-reproduction objective. This is the closest direct neighbor and the one most likely to be cited as "already does the calibrate-and-retrain loop for legged/humanoid sim-to-real"; it does not, however, preempt FCSI's mechanism-interpretability, failure-conditioning, or abstain claims , I did not find language in the fetched abstract suggesting ASAP restricts itself to a small library of physically named parameters or reports per-mechanism posteriors.
- Causal/mechanism attribution of the sim-real gap: **COMPASS (arXiv 2306.15864, preprint)** is the closest neighbor on "which parameter mechanistically explains the gap," using a causal graph rather than a fixed small interpretable library, on manipulation not legged robots, and not failure-conditioned.
- Sparse mechanism-family selection with a complexity penalty: **SINDy** and descendants are the generic template FCSI's step 3 draws on, but applied to algebraic-term libraries for equation discovery, not to a curated physical-mechanism library (friction/Kp-Kd/latency/etc.) for simulator patching, and not failure-conditioned.
- Multiple-shooting / segment-based parameter estimation to avoid trajectory-MSE double-penalty after divergence: well-established general dynamical-systems literature (1991 ACC and later), but I found no robotics sim-to-real paper in this search that operationalizes it as a *calibrated statistical first-divergence detector against a nominal-trajectory discrepancy distribution*, which is FCSI's specific proposal.

I found **no direct duplicate** of FCSI as a whole. The narrow gap that survives in my scope: a method that (i) triggers specifically on real-failure/sim-success trajectories, (ii) detects t_div via calibration against the nominal (non-failure) discrepancy distribution rather than an ad hoc threshold, (iii) searches a small, named, physically interpretable mechanism library (not a generic residual network and not a generic causal graph over all parameters) with an explicit sparsity penalty, (iv) scores candidates on event/failure reproduction rather than trajectory MSE, with an explicit abstain state, and (v) closes the loop with policy retraining, validated on a legged robot. This combination was not found. This is a conservative negative result based on abstracts and, for a subset of papers, fetched abstract text; I did not fetch full methods/appendices for every paper (see caveats below), so I cannot rule out that an appendix of ASAP, NPDR, or COMPASS contains a mechanism-restriction or event-based variant not visible in the abstract.

### Verdict

No direct duplicate. RADIUM and its precursor ("A Bayesian approach to breaking things," now folded into the T-RO version of RADIUM, DOI 10.1109/tro.2025.3551198 , so RADIUM is **PEER-REVIEWED**, not just a preprint) are the closest neighbors in the Chuchu Fan/Charles Dawson lineage, but they search a simulator's own parameter space to *predict where an autonomous system might fail*, with no notion of a real trajectory, no first-divergence localization, no library of interpretable mechanism families, and no sparsity penalty; failure-event matching is not their objective, cost/robustness is. CalNF (arXiv 2502.21110, still PREPRINT, no Crossref/journal record found) is the one paper in this space that explicitly frames the problem as "what can we learn from a single failure," which is structurally close to FCSI's starting condition, but it does calibrated density estimation over failure-inducing parameters, not (a) first-divergence localization against nominal trajectories, (b) a discrete search over named physical mechanism families, (c) a complexity/sparsity penalty, or (d) failure-event reproduction as the fit criterion; its case study is Southwest Airlines scheduling, not a legged robot. "Failure Prediction from Limited Hardware Demonstrations" (arXiv 2410.09249, PREPRINT) is a black-box GP failure classifier trained on real+sim data, not a physics-mechanism hypothesis search. The two legged-robot sim-to-real papers found (Sontakke et al., IROS 2023, residual physics + sysid; Bjelonic et al., IJRR 2026, minimal-parameter actuator-energy sysid) are exactly the "global sysid" baseline that FCSI's own proposal contrasts against: both fit dynamics/energy parameters over ordinary (non-failure) trajectory distributions, with no failure-conditioning, no first-divergence timestamp, no mechanism-family selection, and MSE-like fit rather than failure-event reproduction.

The narrow gap that survives, within the scope actually verified here: a method that (1) starts from one logged real failure where sim predicted success, (2) statistically localizes the first persistent real/sim divergence calibrated on nominal data, (3) searches a small, named library of interpretable mechanism families (friction, anisotropic friction, compliance, actuator strength, gains, latency, saturation, mass, sensor delay) for the sparsest one that reproduces both the divergence timing and the failure event (not just low trajectory MSE), (4) exposes ranked hypotheses/posterior weights/equivalence classes with an explicit abstain option, and (5) retrains a policy on the failure-conditioned repaired simulator on a **legged robot** , was not found as a single paper in the sources checked. This is a conservative claim: it rests on verified abstracts, titles, and (where fetched) full text of the closest neighbors above, not on exhaustive reading of every paper's methods/appendices; RADIUM's and CalNF's supplementary material were not fully read line-by-line, only their abstracts and (for RADIUM) the confirmed T-RO publication metadata, so a downgrade to NOT-VERIFIED on any narrower sub-claim about their appendices would be warranted before treating this as a hard novelty gate pass.
